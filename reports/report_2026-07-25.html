Section 3 — Major Public Company Lawsuits & Regulatory Actions

Elon Musk / SEC — Twitter (X) Disclosure Settlement
The SEC formally settled its long-running enforcement action against Elon Musk stemming from his failure to timely and accurately disclose his accumulating stake in Twitter (now X Corp) in early 2022. Musk was required under Section 13(d) of the Securities Exchange Act to disclose his position within 10 days of crossing the 5% ownership threshold — a deadline he missed by approximately 11 days, during which he continued purchasing shares at artificially uninformed prices, allegedly saving himself hundreds of millions of dollars while other investors sold at lower prices. The settlement imposes a $1.5 million fine — a figure widely criticized by securities law experts as wholly inadequate relative to the alleged benefit received and Musk's net worth, which now exceeds $300 billion. The SEC, under the current administration's deregulatory posture, agreed to terms with no admission of wrongdoing. The case has significant governance implications for large activist positions and sets what many view as a dangerously permissive precedent for billionaire disclosure compliance.

WAMCO (Western Asset Management) / SEC — Cherry-Picking Scheme, $100M Settlement
Western Asset Management Company, formerly one of the largest fixed income investment managers in the world and a subsidiary of Franklin Templeton (BEN), reached a $100 million settlement with the SEC over allegations that portfolio managers engaged in systematic cherry-picking — allocating profitable trades to favored accounts (including personal or affiliated accounts) while directing losing trades to client portfolios. The practice, which allegedly spanned multiple years, represents a fundamental breach of fiduciary duty and investment adviser obligations under the Investment Advisers Act. The $100M penalty is material for Franklin Templeton and arrives at an already difficult time for WAMCO, which has been experiencing significant asset outflows following earlier reputational damage. Financial exposure beyond the fine includes client redemption pressure and potential civil litigation from institutional clients who can now point to the SEC's findings as evidentiary support in private suits.

SEC Corporate Enforcement Posture — Skadden Guidance on Current Administration Settlements
Skadden, Arps, Slate, Meagher & Flom — one of the preeminent securities litigation firms — published significant guidance this week analyzing a pattern of recent SEC public company settlements that reveals the current administration's enforcement philosophy: lower financial penalties, faster resolution timelines, broader deferred prosecution agreement usage, and a marked reduction in individual officer accountability requirements. Congressional Insider Trading Legislation — "Stop Insider Trading Act" Passed House
The House passed the "Stop Insider Trading Act" this week — landmark legislation that would prohibit members of Congress from trading individual stocks while in office, requiring divestiture into blind trusts within 180 days of taking office. The critical and market-relevant caveat: the bill explicitly exempts the President and Vice President from its prohibitions. The legislation now faces an uncertain Senate path and a probable presidential veto threat given the exemption controversy. The broader regulatory implication for markets is the potential shift in congressional trading patterns if the law is eventually enacted — historically, academic research has shown congressional portfolios outperform the S&P 500 by 6-12% annually, a premium that is difficult to explain without information advantages.
